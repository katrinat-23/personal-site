Misophonia trigger detector

This is the sound detection behind the misophonia earbuds I'm building. It listens through your mic and flags trigger sounds like chewing, sniffing, and typing as they happen. Press start, then try crunching on a snack or tapping your keyboard.

Everything runs in your browser. Your mic audio never leaves this tab.

Wear headphones for this mode. On speakers, the mic hears the masking noise and can keep setting itself off. The headphones stand in for the earbuds.

Controls

Sensitivity: higher catches quieter or less certain sounds, but gives more false alarms. The other four only matter in masking mode. Volume sets how loud the noise gets. Fade in and fade out set how quickly it starts and stops. Hold sets how long it keeps going after the last trigger. While the noise is playing, the detector needs to be more confident before it counts a sound, so it doesn't react to its own noise.

Built with Google's YAMNet sound classifier running in TensorFlow.js. The model (about 14MB) and its list of sound labels download once when you press start. No audio is uploaded, and the masking noise is generated in your browser. Works best in Chrome or Safari on a laptop.
